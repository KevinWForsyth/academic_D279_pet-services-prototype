Expert Advice for a Happy, Healthy Dog

The domestic dog is an extremely social animal, offering a diverse variety of choices as there are over 300 breeds recognized by the World Canine Organization. Adopting a dog brings mutual benefits for both the dog and the owner. Many studies highlight the social benefits of having a companion and the reduction of loneliness. Additionally, adopting a dog has medical benefits, such as reduced blood pressure and improved lipid profiles. As a dog owner, it’s essential to care for your dog by providing proper nutrition and ample opportunities for exercise. This page will help you with essential tips on caring for your dog through the various stages of their life.


Dog Care Essentials

Throughout your dog's lifespan, it's crucial to pay attention to their diet, exercise, grooming, and overall health. Let's dive into these important aspects of dog care:


Diet & Exercise

Dogs' nutritional needs change as they age. Puppies should be nursed for the first two months and then transitioned to three meals per day when they are about three to six months old. By the time they reach six months to one year, they should transition to two meals per day, and eventually, to one meal per day after one year. Hydration is just as important; a dog’s typical body mass is composed of 60-70% water, and dehydration can lead to illness or even death. Make sure your dog has access to clean water at all times!


Exercise is key to maintaining your dog’s health. The amount of exercise varies depending on breed and size, but generally, dogs need 30–120 minutes of exercise per day. Exercise doesn’t mean strenuous workouts — brisk walks are great too! You can also use toys to encourage physical activity, but ensure they are safe and age-appropriate for your dog.


Grooming & Handling

Regular grooming is essential for your dog’s well-being. Brush your dog frequently to reduce shedding and prevent mats in the fur. Pay special attention to ticks and fleas, especially after outdoor excursions. Bathing is another important grooming activity, but be cautious with soap products. Make sure to use dog-formulated shampoos to avoid skin irritation, and always rinse thoroughly to remove soap residue.


Proper handling of your dog is essential for both you and your dog’s safety. Whether small or large, be gentle when carrying or lifting your dog. For small dogs, support them under the chest, and for larger dogs, use both arms to support their chest and rear.


Vaccinations & Medications

Consult with your veterinarian for the necessary vaccinations and medications for your dog. Core vaccines typically include those for rabies, hepatitis, and parvovirus. Additional vaccines, such as Bordetella bronchiseptica, Borrelia burgdorferi, and Leptospira bacteria, may be recommended based on your dog's environment and lifestyle.
